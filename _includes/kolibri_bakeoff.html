On 3 October 2026 Aleph Alpha released Kolibri-1, an open model trained with an unusually large share of German. I built Die Kartei, an iPhone app that teaches German with a small AI tutor running on the phone. Its code is public on GitHub, including everything this post describes. Kolibri is far too big for a phone, so my question was narrower: could it write better training examples for my tutor than the model I use now, Google's Gemma 4 31B? I gave both the same tasks. Sadly... it couldn't

The app, the tutor and its teachers

Die Kartei's tutor is a Google Gemma or IBM Granite model, depending on the iPhone: the amount of RAM on the phone limits which model can run. There are three sizes, small, medium and large, all fine-tuned for German. They correct what learners write, chat with them, and write short practice stories.

LLM distillation

Small models learn best from examples, and hand-writing thousands of good German example sentences to train on isn't realistic. So a much larger model writes “synthetic training data”, and the small model trains on the result. LLM researchers call the large model the teacher and the small model the student. Whatever the teacher gets wrong, the student learns as correct, so picking the teacher matters more than almost anything else. The tutor's training code and data recipes are in the repo's training folder.

I asked both candidate teachers to write two kinds of examples. On this page, German appears in this face and English in the regular one.

Task 1 · Kasus stories

German marks a noun's role in the sentence with its article, in four cases. Learners struggle with this more than almost anything else.

Nominative
Der Hund schläft.the subject
Accusative
Ich sehe den Hund.the direct object
Dative
Ich gebe dem Hund einen Knochen.the receiver
Genitive
Der Napf des Hundes ist leer.whose

The app's planner picks five to eight phrases and asks the tutor to write a short story that uses each one exactly as given. A grammar checker built into the app, with no AI in it, then proves every case before the story becomes an exercise.

durch eine Straßeeinen Kaffeeden Brieffür die Omaunter den Tischder Bruder

Max geht durch eine Straße. Er kauft einen Kaffee. Er geht in ein Café. Er liest den Brief. Der Brief ist für die Oma. Max stellt den Brief unter den Tisch. … Der Bruder wartet vor der Tür.

Task 2 · Correction examples

The tutor's main job is correcting learners. Each training example is a sentence with one specific mistake, the fix, and a short explanation. The teacher has to write the mistake it was asked for, not just any mistake.

Asked for: wrong auxiliary verb Ich bin heute leider viel zu lange geschlafen. Ich habe heute leider viel zu lange geschlafen. “I slept far too long today.” Schlafen is a state, not a movement, so it takes haben. (Gemma 31B)
Asked for: wrong auxiliary verb Ich haben letztes Wochenende ein Buch gelesen. Ich habe letztes Wochenende ein Buch gelesen. “I read a book last weekend.” Correct German, but the mistake is subject–verb agreement, not the choice of auxiliary. A student trained on this learns the wrong lesson. (Kolibri-1)

Key findings

93% vs 71%

Gemma 31B writes better stories by every measure. 93% of its stories pass the app's grammar checker, and 99% of the requested phrases appear exactly as given. Kolibri manages 71% and 76%.

5 / 20 vs 9 / 20

The checker misses things a reader catches. I read 20 passing stories from each model. 5 of Gemma's still had a grammar error, and 9 of Kolibri's did.

33% vs 91%

Kolibri often writes the wrong mistake. Asked for sentences with one specific learner error, only a third of its imperative examples and under half of its subjunctive examples had that error.

55× the tokens

Reasoning doesn't rescue Kolibri. Letting it think first lifts its checker score to 89%, at about 55 times the tokens, but I still found errors in 7 of 20 stories.

Four tests for a story

Every story went through four tests. The first two are run by the app's checker, a program that knows German case rules and nothing else. The last two are run by a person reading: I took 20 random stories per model that had passed the checker and read every sentence. Each card shows a real sentence that fails its test.

Put both kinds of test together and the gap gets plain. Out of every 20 stories a teacher writes, this is roughly how many I could train on.

Correction examples

Both models got the same 48 requests, 12 each for four kinds of mistake, eight examples per request. An example counts as the right kind when its mistake is the one asked for, like the bin geschlafen example above. My data validator then throws out malformed and duplicate examples, and ones a grammar checker flags.

Thinking costs tokens

Kolibri can reason before it writes. With room to think, it passed the checker 89% of the time, close to Gemma. But it thought for a median 8,802 tokens per story, and a reader still finds about as many errors. Its medium setting thought for 13,493 tokens and did no better.

Why Gemma writes better German here

Kolibri saw far more German in training than most open models, so I expected it to win. Its German is fluent. Where it falls down is precision: fitting a given phrase into a sentence without breaking the grammar around it, or writing exactly the mistake it was asked for. As far as I can tell, four things explain the result.

99%
of requested phrases kept word for word, against 76% for Kolibri

Gemma follows the plan

Gemma kept 99% of the planned phrases exactly as given; Kolibri changed or dropped about a quarter. When Kolibri did use a phrase, it often bent the sentence to fit it, and that's where its case errors sit. A phrase planned for movement, unter das Bett (“under the bed”, as in putting something there), ends up beside a verb of position like liegt (“lies”), which needs dem Bett.

31B vs 3.5B
parameters working on each word

Every word gets the whole model

Gemma 31B is dense: all 31 billion parameters work on every word it writes. Kolibri is a mixture of experts. For each word, a router picks 6 of 384 small expert networks per layer, so only about 3.5 of its 78 billion parameters do the work. That makes it fast and cheap to run.

My working explanation is that this also costs precision on tasks like these. I saw the same pattern earlier with Google's own mixture-of-experts model, Gemma 4 26B-A4B, which has about 4 billion active. With the same prompt, it wrote correctly built reflexive-pronoun mistakes 12% of the time, where the dense 31B managed 100%. Two tests aren't proof, but they point the same way.

9 of 20
of Kolibri's passing stories had a grammar error, against 5 of 20 for Gemma

Fluent isn't the same as correct

Kolibri's mistakes aren't clumsy word choices. They're structural: the wrong case after a preposition, haben where German needs sein. Those are exactly what a learner opens the app to get right, which makes them the worst kind of mistake for a teacher to have.

55×
the tokens per story with reasoning on, for 89% on the checker

Thinking longer didn't fix it

Reasoning raised Kolibri's checker score from 71% to 89%, so it helped it satisfy the rules the checker knows. It didn't make the German cleaner, and each story took about 55 times as many tokens.

5.4 min
for 306 stories on one H200, with standard tools

Gemma is also simpler to run

Gemma 31B runs with standard tools on any 80 GB GPU and wrote 306 stories in 5.4 minutes. Kolibri needs one specific vLLM version with Aleph Alpha's plugin and a machine with a recent CUDA driver, and it crashed on its first start until I switched off one of vLLM's sampling kernels.

Where Kolibri did well

It's fast: 306 stories in 30 seconds once loaded, against Gemma's 5.4 minutes. Its negation examples had the right mistake 97% of the time. And it brings strengths this test doesn't use: German-first training, a 262K-token context and an Apache 2.0 license. None of that outweighs its error rate for this job.

Caveats

  • One evening, one run per model. The hand reads are 20 stories each.
  • The prompts, the checker and the measurements are mine, and I built them over months alongside Gemma models. That may favor Gemma.
  • Each model ran with its maker's recommended sampling settings. Kolibri was a day old, on a brand-new serving stack.
  • These are narrow tasks: writing stories around fixed phrases, and writing specific learner mistakes. They say nothing about Kolibri as a chat model, on long documents, or in English.

What's next

Even Gemma's stories aren't ready to train on: 5 of 20 had an error. Most of those trace back to my side. The app's planner pairs prepositions and nouns at random (für die Karte, “for the map”), shows verbs in the present tense even when a story is in the past, and its checker misses a few patterns. I'm fixing those and adding a second grammar check. Then Gemma 31B writes a few thousand stories for the next round of tutor training, about an hour on one GPU.

German open models I'm watching

I'm excited to see what Kolibri 1.1 looks like: Aleph Alpha's Co-CEO Ilhan Scheer wrote that it's already in the works. I also hope Aleph Alpha puts Kolibri on OpenRouter, which would make tests like this one possible without renting a GPU.

I'm looking forward to Soofi S too, a German and English foundation model built by the Lamarr Institute, Fraunhofer IAIS, DFKI, TU Darmstadt, hessian.AI, ellamind, the L3S Research Center and the University of Würzburg, coordinated by the German AI Association (KI Bundesverband) and funded by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy. It's in closed beta for now. Like Kolibri it's a mixture of experts, with about 3.2 billion of its 31.6 billion parameters active per word, so I'm curious how it does on these same tests.

And I'm excited by what Google and IBM keep releasing with Gemma and Granite. Their small open models are what make an offline German tutor on an iPhone possible at all.

Congratulations, Aleph Alpha

Kolibri is released under Apache 2.0, so anyone can download it, inspect it and build on it, including in an app like mine. That isn't a given for German-first models: LLäMmlein 7B and 1B from the University of Würzburg are licensed for research use only, and Fraunhofer's ELMOD 2.7B is non-commercial (CC BY-NC 4.0). Kolibri didn't win this bake-off, but it's a model I can actually use, and I'll put 1.1 through the same tests.
