Takeaways so far

  1. Evaluate before you train. Half of my planned training areas turned out not to need training. The eval also revealed that my most valuable slice of data would be correct sentences — which no intuition about "grammar weaknesses" would have produced.
  2. Behavioral failures beat knowledge failures. The model mostly knows N-Deklination; it fails at verdict discipline — knowing when to say "this is fine." That's a fine-tuning problem, not a knowledge problem, and it's cheap to fix with data.
  3. Bench the app, not the checkpoint. Twice, my scorer measured a model no user would ever meet: once flattering a "failed" fine-tune into the bin (the E2B echo artifact), once flattering a broken incumbent onto the scoreboard (the "58%" that was actually silence). Implement production's full parse contract in the eval, and treat scores as a cache over raw generations — a scorer fix must be replayed over saved results.
  4. Synthetic data works if the teacher is strong and the gate is real — and the gate has to check shape, not just validity. The cheap-teacher A/B failed invisibly (42% wrong labels), and at 40k scale a one-line regex deleted every actual correction while the pass rate went up. Validity gates catch bad rows; only a composition gate catches missing ones.
  5. Below a capacity floor, fine-tuning subtracts — at any data scale. The same recipes that lifted the 4B model cut Gemma 3 1B nearly in half at 1,400 examples and again at 40,000. Small models learn the confident behavior without the knowledge to aim it; more data buys capability at 4B, caution at ~2B, and nothing at 1B. The structural reason lives in the vocabulary-vs-body budget split above.
  6. Measurement beats vivid examples. One glance at "Funktionieren Sie als Softwareingenieur" convinced me the translated mix-in had to go; the A/B said removing it costs 7 points of core accuracy. When a bad-looking sample suggests a data decision, run the comparison before believing your eyes.
  7. On-device is not a compromise for language learning — it's an advantage. Faster turns, total privacy for your fumbling attempts at a new language, works offline, and zero per-user marginal cost, which changes what kind of app you can afford to build.
  8. Measure the substrate, then measure how much of it is spendable. A stock score tells you what a base knows; it doesn't tell you how much room is left to teach it anything. The models that spend a third to a half of their weights on a vocabulary table started ahead on German and had nowhere to put a new habit, while the IBM Granites started last and were the only bases in the survey that swallowed the whole corpus and got better for it. Thirty minutes of evals beats any model card — and knowing where the parameters went beats both.
