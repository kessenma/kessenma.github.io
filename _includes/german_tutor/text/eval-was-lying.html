The eval was lying to me

Instead of asking how many false corrections the tuned E2B made, I decomposed how each one fails. The answer changed the whole project:

An "echo" is the model emitting FIX: <the student's sentence, verbatim> plus a confabulated reason. Of the tuned E2B's 19 false corrections, 18 were echoes. Its only genuinely wrong rewrite in the whole 121-item suite was swapping zumachen for zuschlagen — "close" for "slam" — on one sentence. And here's the punchline: the app's correction parser has discarded echoes since before any of this training work existed. It compares the FIX line against the student's original and returns "all clear" when they match. The app never showed a user a single one of those 18 echoed corrections. My eval harness simply didn't model that step — so every false-correction number on the scoreboard measured the raw checkpoint rather than the shipped behavior. The 59% was real as a statement about the checkpoint, and misleading as a statement about the app.

Mirroring the app's parse contract into the scorer (the app guard) and re-scoring the saved responses — a pure post-process, no re-inference, verified across 10 models × 121 items with zero regressions — redrew the board. Two scales now exist: raw measures the checkpoint, guarded measures what a user actually experiences. Guarded is the honest one, and every number from here on is guarded:

The tuned E2B doesn't teeter on any cliff. It beats stock E2B by +10 points of core accuracy and 9 points of miss rate, at the cost of one bad correction in 32. What the 2B had actually learned was the FIX format — including the habit of using it to say "no change needed" — while its knowledge gains were real all along. A formatting failure with a two-line fix, not a capacity ceiling. (Gemma 3 1B produced zero echoes, so the 1B collapse survives re-scoring untouched — the cliff is real; E2B was just never on it.)

Building the guard also fixed two real defects in the app's own filter: it compared diacritic-insensitively, silently swallowing genuine umlaut corrections (Madchen → Mädchen is exactly what a learner needs to see), and it demanded an exact match otherwise, so an echo that merely added a period leaked through as a spurious correction. Both fixed. A memory measurement closed the loop: the model's true resident peak is 2.71 GB — the app's old estimate scaled the download size and overshot by 40%, which was the only thing excluding 6 GB phones. So on 2026-07-22 the fine-tune I had written off shipped as the 6 GB tier, public on Hugging Face (kessenma/gemma4-e2b-german-tutor-4bit) with the echo filter published right in the model card — without it, the raw model reads as a 59%-false-correction disaster.

Bench the app, not the checkpoint. My eval mirrored the model's output format but not the app's parse contract, and the gap between those two nearly killed a shippable model. Implement every step of what production does with a reply — filters, guards, fallbacks — or the bench measures a model the user never meets. This lesson gets a second, crueler confirmation below.
