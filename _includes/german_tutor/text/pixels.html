The same question, in pixels

The tutor isn't the only model on the phone. Short Stories can illustrate themselves and flashcards can draw their vocabulary — through a second on-device model, a CoreML Stable Diffusion 2.1 pipeline at about 1.2 GB. It comes with its own memory choreography: the illustrator only loads after the ~5 GB tutor has been evicted from memory (they can't coexist on a 6 GB phone), and it has to abort cleanly the instant the user hits Stop. Picking it was a rerun of the language track's core lesson in different clothes: every "best image model" thread on the internet points at 8–24B models that assume a desktop GPU, and the phone has its own physics — so you ignore the leaderboards and measure what actually fits. The encouraging coda is also the same: Apple's next OS ships an official on-device export of a frontier-class four-billion-parameter image model, so on both tracks — words and pixels — "what runs on a phone" is a line that moves in only one direction, and faster than you'd guess.
