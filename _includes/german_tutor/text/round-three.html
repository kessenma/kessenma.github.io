Round three: the hunt for a 4 GB tutor

The 6 GB tier was solved. One rung remained: 4 GB devices — the iPhone XR / 11 / SE class, exactly the "older phone in a drawer" a budget learner is most likely to own. The incumbent there was stock Gemma 3 1B, on the books as a respectable 58% core with a reassuring 0% false corrections: "safe but limited." Re-checking that anchor with the repaired scorer turned up a second, crueler version of the same bug. My guard implemented the app's echo rule but not the rest of its parse contract, and Gemma 3 1B answers nearly every prompt with OK followed by a FIX: line — a shape the app renders as nothing at all. Scored the way the app behaves, the incumbent is 34% core with a 100% miss rate. Its celebrated 0% false-correction rate was never caution; it was silence. Flushing it out meant re-scoring every saved result from its raw generations — scores are a cache, generations are the artifact.

Two escape hatches closed quickly. Shrinking the models I already had doesn't work: Gemma 4's per-layer embeddings — the trick that makes it memory-efficient at 4-bit — must stay high-precision or the model breaks, so a mixed 3-bit build of the tuned E2B lands at 3.2 GB, barely under the 4-bit's 3.4 GB, while cratering core accuracy from 83% to 63%. There is no real 3-bit tier beneath Gemma 4's "4-bit." And the off-the-shelf field had no answer either: every plausible sub-3B candidate went through the same guarded eval and failed one of two ways — the German-first models won't render a verdict, and the format-disciplined ones don't know enough German. Only IBM's Granite 3.3 2B cleared the true floor, at 55%: the best-behaved non-Gemma measured, Apache-licensed, 1.4 GB.

So round three became a controlled experiment at the floor: take the new v2 corpus — ~40,000 validated examples against round one's ~1,400 — and give it to two very different students. Gemma 3 1B, the best German substrate available under a gigabyte. And Granite 3.3 2B, the worst German of anything I'd still consider, but a very different shape of model.

The 1B result settles the capacity cliff for good: 28× the data made it worse again (34% → 26%, and it started inventing corrections). The cliff was never about data volume. Granite absorbed the same corpus cleanly — more than doubling what the app ships to a 4 GB phone — and then stopped well short of the 6 GB tier, and a rank test says that ceiling isn't a tuning problem: quadrupling the adapter drove validation loss down hard (0.89 → 0.71) and moved the benchmark by four items out of 82, an exact McNemar p = 0.42. What the extra capacity bought instead was verdict discipline — false corrections to zero, the metric this project treats as the trust-killer. Judgment, not knowledge.

Two ways to spend a small parameter budget

The split between those two students is the whole story, and it's architectural. A language model's parameters go into two pots: the vocabulary table that maps text to tokens, and the body that does the thinking. In a big model the table is a rounding error. In a small one it is the dominant cost — and it is also the pot that fine-tuning can't use.
