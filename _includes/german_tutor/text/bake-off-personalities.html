Model families have personalities — and they're inverse

This was the finding I didn't expect. What came back from the survey wasn't really a ranking. There are exactly two ways to be a bad German tutor, and each model family picks one:

The over-correctors have a judgment deficit. Both Gemma 4 models fail this way: over-eager corrections of sentences that were already right. Other families take it to an extreme — Ministral 8B "fixed" 84% of the perfectly correct sentences shown to it, and Aya Expanse 8B, Cohere's multilingual flagship, corrected all 32 of them. It knows German; it just can't say "this is fine."

The rubber-stampers have a knowledge deficit. Every Qwen fails the opposite way: waving broken German through as OK. Qwen3-8B falsely corrects only 6% of the time — a great-looking number — but misses 48% of real errors. Phi-4 Mini misses 77%. Llama 3.2 1B took it to the logical conclusion and answered OK to all 69 errors in the suites: a perfect false-correction score, achieved by never correcting anything.

The trap is that a model that never says "this is fine" and a model that never says "this is wrong" score surprisingly similarly on a naive accuracy metric — and are equally useless as tutors. That's why these two behavioral rates, not the headline score, became my primary metrics, and why the bottom-left corner of the chart above is the only place worth being. And if you have to pick a failure mode: the over-corrector is annoying, but the rubber-stamper is fatal. A student can recover from being told a correct sentence was wrong. A student can't learn from a teacher who validates their mistakes.

The model already on the phone is the worst tutor of all. The obvious question about an on-device German app in 2026 is "why ship your own model — the iPhone already has Apple Intelligence." So I ran it through the identical eval (through its FoundationModels Swift API instead of MLX, same prompts, same scorer). Apple's ~3B on-device model was the most extreme over-corrector in the whole survey: it "fixed" all 32 correct sentences — a 100% false-correction rate — by echoing the student's sentence back unchanged under a FIX: header with a made-up reason ("Incorrect subject-verb agreement needed" on the flawless Sie wartet auf ihren Freund). It never once said "this is fine." Its grammar knowledge is Mistral-tier — 42% on the core four, 0/15 on da-/wo-compounds — and tellingly it made zero format errors: it has learned the shape of a correction task perfectly and has none of the judgment to know when not to apply it.

The meta-lesson: none of this was visible before building the eval. Every one of these models ships with a beautiful model card and strong multilingual benchmark numbers — Phi-4 Mini in particular is a benchmark darling that turned in Mistral-tier German. The whole bake-off cost nothing but Mac compute time, because the harness already existed. Evals are the cheapest instrument in the on-device toolchain, and the highest-leverage one.
