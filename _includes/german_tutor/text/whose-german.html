Whose German?

One question I never thought to evaluate until everything had already shipped: which German is this. I learned mine in Vienna, and German is pluricentric — Germany, Austria and Switzerland each maintain a codified standard — so Semmel, Jänner and Marille aren't slang, they're correct Austrian standard German. Every item in my eval was written in the German standard, and so, it turns out, is the corpus — not by decision. No generation prompt ever named a variety; level and formality were the only register controls I gave the teacher, and the monoculture emerged anyway. Scanning 3.6M tokens of v2 training answers against 35 codified markers: the fourteen Austrian words hit twice against 1,434 German counterparts, the eight Swiss ones zero times. The model has seen Kartoffel 244 times and Erdapfel never. The one regional voice the data does carry is northern German — Moin opens 313 conversation rows.

Which would be trivia, except the app ships a correction mode: a tutor that marks correct Austrian German as an error is broken in a way no grammar eval in this repo can see, because every eval item was written in Bundesdeutsch. So I ran eight valid Austrian/Swiss sentences through the app's real correction contract, with three German twins and two genuinely broken sentences as controls:

ModelValid AT/CH sentences flagged as errors
Gemma 4 E4B stock5 / 8
Gemma 4 E4B v21 / 8
Granite 3.3 2B stock5 / 8
Granite 3.3 2B tuned3 / 8

Stock E4B rewrites Ich bin gestern lange im Café gesessen into Präteritum and Velo into Fahrrad; on one sentence it swaps the verb outright, gestanden → gewartet. The fine-tune leaves all of that alone — but the honest reading is mixed, because it also waved through a genuinely broken control (eine Semmeln), and its miss rate is higher than v1's, so some of that tolerance is plain permissiveness rather than variety awareness. The counter-evidence that some of it is real: on the other broken control it fixed the word-order error while leaving bin … gesessen untouched — exactly what a variety-aware tutor should do — where stock rewrote the whole sentence into Bundesdeutsch. Fine-tuning also subtracts here: stock E4B writes passable Bavarian on request ("I mog di vü und mia gsehn uns morgn"), while the tune answers all four dialect prompts in flat Standard German. And the substrate lesson repeats one last time — a newer Granite 4.1 3B, stock, is the only model in the study that performs the Austrian perfect on request (Die Katze ist auf dem Sofa gelegen), and its tune loses that too. Neither Gemma manages it in either direction, which is a little embarrassing given the app's own sein/haben guide teaches exactly that contrast. Thirty-five markers are a sample, not a census, and none of this had native-speaker validation — but it's enough to say the tuning made the tutor safer for Austrian learners and narrower for everyone curious about how anyone else speaks.
