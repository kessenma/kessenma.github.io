What a real learner found

Everything so far was measured on an eval bench. Benches don't get frustrated, don't crash, and never wonder where a setting lives — so before pushing the models further, I recruited a fellow German learner in Vienna (A1, studying at a language school here) to spend real time with the app and answer a structured set of questions. Their answers are below verbatim, lightly cleaned up for typos, because the unfiltered phrasing turned out to be the most useful data in this article.

Flashcards — with and without the image-generation settings?

"All these worked fine. The German quality is nice, so no doubt on it. The UX is also good for me."

The reading + audio stories?

"So I got the topic Einkaufen im Supermarkt — it was pretty nice. The content and the questions were OK, but while reading it looked more like an AI voice. I mean, it's not a bug or something, but it looked too much AI generated — if it can have some better voice it will be nice. Also generating paragraphs took a while. There were no crashes anywhere."

"By AI generated I also mean: if there could have been a better expression, the conversation could have been more interesting. And not every time would I be interested in sharing my voice data. Also scanning a photo took a while."

The speaking, turn-based conversations?

"I started with A2 and it worked completely fine, but when I changed the language level to A1 it crashed once — after the app started again it worked fine. I even tried talking on some other topic but it still remained the same, so it was fine. The available settings were good, and made it easier to try different approaches."

The learner tools inside a conversation — double-tapping unknown words, saving phrases, response hints?

"I tried 8–10 words and there was no response-hint delay; double-tapping unknown words and adding saved phrases also worked pretty well."

"Initially I didn't know how to add different voices, but later found it in the settings."

Having a conversation based on a photo or URL?

"I did not get this option."

The job-interview conversation mode?

"The first time it crashed; the second time it worked. Also — I studied A1 at a school in Vienna, and I do believe that for the A1 level, the grammar the job interview uses is a little hard."

Navigation — home, activities, library, settings?

"The home section is nice, but under 'Your Streak' it would be good to select the calendar by month, week or year — and the 'This week' section below could also let me pick the range, from and until when I want to see."

"All-activities looks good, but if you don't want the user to scroll a long page you could make a dropdown per section, or maybe six main cards (vocab, grammar, reading, speaking, …) that navigate to a sub-page — but this can be a little complicated for developers, as it changes the interface."

"Library: works perfectly fine. Settings: the tab system is working, still good, no change needed."

The model-selection screen?

"The explanation is good enough for anyone to understand — but if a user just selects what they need and a model is then chosen automatically, I think it would be more user-friendly. As a user I'm not really interested in checking all the descriptions of the models."

Sorting that feedback taught me it comes in three distinct kinds. Some of it is not the tutor model at all: the "AI voice" is Apple's on-device text-to-speech reading the story aloud — no amount of fine-tuning touches that. Some of it is app work, now queued: the A1 crash, the interview mode both crashing once and speaking above an A1 learner's level (a CEFR-adherence gap, which is a model behavior), the photo-conversation feature they never found, calendar ranges, and picking a model for the user instead of asking them to read spec sheets — that last one is exactly what the device-tier table at the end of this article does. (Their instinct about "sharing my voice data" is also the anxiety the on-device design already answers — speech never leaves the phone — which means the app is failing to say so.) But the middle of their story feedback — "if there could have been a better expression, the conversation could have been more interesting" — is squarely about the language model. The tutor was grammatically solid and conversationally flat. My eval measured correctness and had no opinion at all about that. The next round of training needed to fix both.
