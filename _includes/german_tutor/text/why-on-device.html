Why on-device

When I researched similar tutor apps, all of them I found rely on a server-side model — which comes with additional costs, can be slower, and usually means a subscription fee just to keep your stored progress. Meanwhile, efficiency advances from the frontier labs, smartphone and chip makers building around on-device acceleration, and governments plus the open-source community producing large German datasets have made it increasingly viable to run the model on the phone itself. The surprising part is that on-device is faster in practice: no network round trip, no queueing behind other users — first tokens start streaming from local silicon almost immediately, which matters enormously in a turn-based spoken conversation where a two-second pause feels like talking to a wall. And it's private by construction: your fumbling attempts at a new language never leave the device.

I've built other on-device AI apps, and my honest read of the current state is that language tasks are the sweet spot. Conversation, translation, grammar correction, structured generation — a 4B-class model handles these well enough to ship. Heavier patterns like RAG over large document sets aren't really feasible on-device yet; the retrieval infrastructure, context lengths, and memory pressure fight you at every step. But a tutor is almost a pure language task, which makes it one of the best fits for on-device AI today. And these models are advancing fast — Gemma's E-series roughly doubled in usable quality within a year while staying phone-sized.
